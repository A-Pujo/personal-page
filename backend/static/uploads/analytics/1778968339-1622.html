Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

A mathematical journey from high-dimensional data to its most essential components.

What is PCA?

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is a dimensionality-reduction technique that transforms a large set of variables into a smaller one that still contains most of the information in the large set. It identifies the "principal components"—the directions where there is the most variance in the data.

1

Standardization

The purpose of this step is to standardize the range of the continuous initial variables so that each of them contributes equally to the analysis. If one variable has a much larger range than another, it might dominate the results.

For each feature $j$ and data point $i$, we calculate the Z-score:

$$z = \frac{x_{ij} - \mu_j}{\sigma_j}$$

Where $\mu_j$ is the mean of feature $j$ and $\sigma_j$ is the standard deviation.

2

Covariance Matrix Computation

The covariance matrix is a $p \times p$ symmetric matrix (where $p$ is the number of dimensions) that has as entries the covariances associated with all possible pairs of the initial variables.

$$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = \frac{1}{n-1} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (X_i - \bar{X})(Y_i - \bar{Y})$$

The resulting matrix $\Sigma$ looks like this:

$$\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix} \text{Var}(X_1) & \text{Cov}(X_1, X_2) & \dots \\ \text{Cov}(X_2, X_1) & \text{Var}(X_2) & \dots \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots \end{pmatrix}$$
3

Compute Eigenvectors & Eigenvalues

Eigenvectors and eigenvalues are the linear algebra concepts we need to determine from the covariance matrix in order to determine the principal components of the data.

  • Eigenvectors determine the directions of the new feature space.
  • Eigenvalues determine their magnitude (how much variance is explained by that component).

We solve the characteristic equation:

$$\det(\Sigma - \lambda I) = 0$$

Then find the corresponding eigenvectors $v$ for each eigenvalue $\lambda$:

$$\Sigma v = \lambda v$$
4

Feature Vector Creation

In this step, we choose whether to keep all the components or discard those of lesser importance (low eigenvalues). We sort eigenvalues in descending order:

$$\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_p$$

We then form a Feature Vector (matrix of vectors) by taking the eigenvectors we want to keep ($k$ dimensions):

$$W = [v_1, v_2, \dots, v_k]$$
5

Recast Data Along Principal Components

The final step is to use the feature vector $W$ to reorient the data from the original axes to the ones represented by the principal components.

$$Y = X_{standardized} \times W$$

Where $Y$ is the new reduced dataset, $X_{standardized}$ is our initial standardized data, and $W$ is our matrix of selected eigenvectors.
